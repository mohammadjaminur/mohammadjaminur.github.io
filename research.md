  <h1>Research themes</h1>
  <p class="lede">Two questions I keep coming back to: how do you catch a threat that's built to look ordinary, and what does it cost — to people, and to the planet — to run the AI systems we're increasingly relying on? Featured papers live on <a href="{{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}">Publications</a>.</p>

  <ul class="highlight-list">
    <li>
      <strong>Network security <span style="font-weight:500;color:var(--muted)">(with Dr.&nbsp;Michalis Faloutsos)</span></strong>
      <p>Malware command-and-control (C2) infrastructure is built to blend into legitimate traffic. My work looks for the behavioral and spatial-temporal tells that give it away — patterns in timing, structure, and network position that persist even when the payload changes — so defenders can catch C2 channels that signature-based tools miss.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Responsible AI <span style="font-weight:500;color:var(--muted)">(with Dr.&nbsp;Shaolei Ren)</span></strong>
      <p>The other side of building AI systems is what they cost to run responsibly. This thread measures those costs directly — the environmental footprint of generative-AI inference across cloud versus edge deployments — and designs around them, with equity-aware spatial-temporal workload shifting that keeps data centers sustainable without shifting the burden onto disadvantaged regions.</p>
    </li>
  </ul>
